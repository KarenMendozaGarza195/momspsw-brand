The Brand

Our Story

Moumie grew up in Guinea Conakry, in a culture that treats caring for elders as a privilege, not a duty. She cared for her own mother for 30 years. After working as a PSW in Canada and seeing an overwhelmed system that left seniors rushed and unseen, she founded Mom’s PSW in 2023 to offer something different: compassionate, attentive, bilingual care across Eastern Ontario.

Mom’s PSW is also a Living Wage Canada employer. Fairness extends to the team, not just the clients.

Mission

Redefine caregiving as an act of devotion — uplifting seniors with compassion, dignity, and personalized support while giving PSWs respect, fair pay, and renewed purpose.

Vision

A future where every senior feels loved, supported, and safe — and every PSW feels proud and valued.

What Makes Us Different

Most PSW agencies use the same set of muted, reassuring colours and clinical stock photography. They say “compassionate care” but look and feel like every other agency. Mom’s PSW is deliberately anti-clinical. The brand should feel like the kind of care it delivers: unhurried, personal, and dignified.

The visual identity is culturally grounded in Moumie’s story. The anchor colour, Deep Indigo, is drawn from the Lepi — a traditional Guinean textile dyed in layers of indigo. In Guinean culture, indigo represents trust, happiness, and prosperity: “richness of life.” That phrase is the heartbeat of this brand.

Who We Serve

The People Behind the Brand

Every design decision in this brand comes back to three real people. When you’re building a post, a flyer, or a card, you’re speaking to one of them. Knowing who they are is the difference between a template and something that connects.

Sandra
The Adult Caregiver

49, works full-time, two kids at home. Her mother, 81, lives alone 20 minutes away and needs more help than Sandra can give around her job and family. She’s been quietly picking up the slack for over a year. Asking for outside help feels like admitting she can’t manage — or worse, like she’s outsourcing her mother.

“Will this person care about my mom as much as I do?”

Lucienne
The Senior

80, widowed, lives independently. Bilingual, French-dominant. Proud of her independence and wary of strangers in her home. She doesn’t want to be treated as a task list — she wants companionship, someone who remembers her stories and speaks her language.

“Will I still feel like this is my home?”

Aminata
The PSW

34, certified PSW, bilingual. Good at her job and genuinely cares, but her current employer treats her caseload like a logistics problem — back-to-back visits, no time to connect with clients, and pay that doesn’t reflect the work.

“Is this somewhere I can give the kind of care I got into this for?”

Sandra is the primary audience. She’s the one doing the research, usually in a stressful moment. The brand needs to reassure her that she’s making the right call — warmth that reads as real, professionalism that feels confident without being cold, and a sense that this company has been where she is.

Personality

How We Sound

The brand archetype is Caregiver + Everyman. Steady, calm, genuinely caring — families trust us. Approachable, empathetic, meeting people where they are. Design should feel warm but grounded. Not clinical. Not precious.

Compassionate

We lead with empathy, not expertise. We understand what families are going through because we’ve been there.

Approachable

Warm and easy, never corporate. We talk like a real person, not a brochure.

Reassuring

Calm confidence. We don’t oversell — we show up, and the care speaks for itself.

Trustworthy

Professional without being stiff. We earn trust through consistency, transparency, and follow-through.

The range this brand needs to hold: the logo should feel equally at home on a business card left with a hospital discharge coordinator and on a heartfelt card left with a family. Same brand, flexed in tone. One system, not split personalities.

The Garden

Our Colours

Every colour in this palette is named for something growing and tended — like the people the brand cares for. This isn’t just a visual choice. Each colour represents a real person in Mom’s PSW’s world.

The palette was deliberately pulled back from the bright, fully saturated colours common in franchise-style healthcare branding. Those colours read well at a glance, but they also look like every other agency. Softer, more considered tones move the brand into the register of independent, personal businesses — the kind of colour you’d find in a hand-dyed textile, not a logo generator.

The Key Rules

1

Evergreen Disappears on Dark

Evergreen on Deep Indigo is nearly invisible. Whenever the background is dark, swap Evergreen for New Leaf and switch to the dark version of the logo.

2

Two Oranges, Two Jobs

Orange Marigold is for large headlines and accents only. When orange needs to carry smaller text, use Deep Marigold instead — it’s darker and easier to read.

3

The Oranges Swap on Dark

On light backgrounds, Deep Marigold is the readable one. On Deep Indigo, it flips — Orange Marigold works for large text, Deep Marigold doesn’t. On Evergreen, neither orange works at all — use Pressed Petal.

4

Light Colours Are Backgrounds

Pressed Petal, Chamomile, and New Leaf are surfaces that dark text sits on. None of them work as text colours against each other or against white.

Typography

Our Fonts

Two typefaces, each with a clear job. Together, they read the way Moumie works: warm and capable in the same breath.

Fraunces — Headings & Display

Caring for those who cared for us

Fraunces carries the personality. It’s a warm serif with character — the kind of typeface that feels considered, not default. Use it for headlines, pull quotes, and any moment that needs to land emotionally.

Keep the “wonky” setting turned off. Those playful alternate letters are charming, but they cost readability that this audience can’t spare.

Public Sans — Body & Everything Else

Mom’s PSW was founded to redefine caregiving as an act of devotion — uplifting seniors with compassion, dignity, and personalized support while giving PSWs respect, fair pay, and renewed purpose. The brand is bilingual, because language access is care.

Public Sans handles all the reading: body copy, captions, labels, navigation. It’s clean, steady at small sizes, and works well in French (which runs about 15–20% longer than English — always shrink the layout to fit, never the type).

Rules for Type

Aa

Minimum 16px for Body Text

The audience is older adults. The default size in this system is 18px, not the usual 16. Anything smaller than 16px is only for legal lines and photo credits. This applies to the French versions too.

¶

Give Text Room to Breathe

Tight line spacing on long paragraphs is the single most common readability problem for this audience. Keep generous spacing between lines, and don’t let text run wider than about 65 characters.

Built In, Not Bolted On

Accessibility

Accessibility for this brand is not a compliance checkbox. The people using Mom’s PSW’s materials — seniors, family members under stress, PSWs on their phones between visits — need things to be legible, clear, and easy to use. Every pairing in the colour system has been measured for readability.

18px
Default Text Size
Larger than standard. Set for older eyes.
16px
Smallest Allowed
Nothing smaller unless it’s fine print.
48px
Minimum Button Size
Big enough for anyone to tap comfortably.
EN/FR
Always Bilingual
French is not optional. Lucienne’s first language.

In plain terms: if someone has to squint, zoom in, or guess what a button does, we’ve failed. Every colour pairing, every font size, and every button in this system was chosen with Lucienne in mind. If it works for her, it works for everyone.

Identity

Our Logo

The logo comes in five formats, each designed for a different space. Use the one that fits best — don’t stretch or crop one format to fit where another belongs.

HorizontalBest for headers, email signatures, and wide spaces
StackedPosters, vertical layouts, business cards
BadgeSquare spaces, social profile pictures, circles
IconFavicons, small marks, pattern elements
WordmarkWhen the mark isn’t needed — text only

Light and Dark Backgrounds

The logo has two colour versions. On light backgrounds (Chamomile, White, Pressed Petal), use FullColor — the mark is in Evergreen. On dark backgrounds (Deep Indigo, Evergreen), use FullColorDark — the mark swaps to New Leaf so it stays visible.

This swap matters. Evergreen on Deep Indigo is nearly invisible — the two colours are too close in darkness. Always check which version you’re using.
